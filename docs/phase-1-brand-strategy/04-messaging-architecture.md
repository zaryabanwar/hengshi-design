# Messaging Architecture

## Use of this artifact

This architecture gives content, UX, SEO, and design teams a shared message order
for every approved route family. The example lines are working strategy language,
not publication-approved copy. Every substantive statement still follows the
claims ledger and D-019 publication workflow.

This artifact elaborates Direction A. After a clean independent review, it may
guide downstream definition only if the founder approves Direction A. A Direction
B or C preference requires bounded reconciliation of this message hierarchy,
deterministic revalidation, and a new independent review before use.

## Site-wide hierarchy

1. **Entity:** Hengshi Design.
2. **Category:** Evidence-led innovation delivery partner.
3. **Promise:** From complex ambition to accountable delivery.
4. **Explanation:** Connect the disciplines and evidence needed to move from a
   consequential decision toward delivery.
5. **Pillars:** Frame what matters; connect the system; prove before promising;
   design for every route.
6. **Context:** Exact service, sector, proof, expertise, or trust topic.
7. **Action:** Book a 30-minute discovery conversation, or take a
   context-appropriate secondary action.

## Global CTA language

- **Primary CTA:** Book a 30-minute discovery conversation.
- **Internal outcome:** A verified, consented, confirmed 30-minute qualified
  discovery booking.
- **Qualification explanation:** Share your organization, role, desired outcome,
  relevant service wing, timing, email, and consent; budget is optional. Email
  ownership is verified before slot selection, and the booking is confirmed only
  after durable provider reconciliation.
- **Boundary:** The conversation explores context and potential fit. It is not a
  binding scope, price, timeline, legal term, or delivery commitment.
- **Alternatives:** Explore services, review verified evidence, inspect Trust,
  use available approved help, or contact Hengshi Design.
- **Access rule:** Booking must be directly available without AI, human chat,
  WebGL, audio, an account, an upload, or proof review.

## Home message sequence

The homepage should answer the minimum buyer questions in this order:

1. **Identity and category:** “Hengshi Design is an evidence-led innovation
   delivery partner.”
2. **Promise:** “From complex ambition to accountable delivery.”
3. **Audience relevance:** Name the responsibility areas and explain that
   programs are shaped around an outcome and operating context.
4. **Connected capability:** Introduce all five wings without collapsing the ten
   formal services.
5. **Sector focus:** Agriculture first; mining second.
6. **Evidence choice:** Link verified Work if available, Demos as a separate
   owned-capability collection, and Insights/Experts/Trust according to evidence
   readiness.
7. **Experience choice:** Offer Quick Access and optional immersive exploration
   as equal paths.
8. **Action:** Invite the qualified 30-minute discovery booking with the visitor
   CTA “Book a 30-minute discovery conversation.”

If proof collections are empty, use an approved honest state and route to reviewed
methods, Demos, Insights, or discovery. Do not create placeholder logos, cases,
metrics, or expert cards to fill the layout.

## Services hierarchy

### Services hub

**Message:** Hengshi Design connects five capability wings around the outcome,
evidence, and operating constraints that matter. The hub helps a buyer compare
the exact services; it does not imply that every service is always included.

### Wing pages

Each wing page should explain:

- the decision domain the wing addresses;
- its exact contained formal services;
- how it connects to other wings;
- typical questions and boundaries, after specialist review;
- relevant agriculture/mining context where evidence exists;
- approved Work, Demos, Insights, Experts, and Trust links; and
- a discovery action with the wing prefilled but editable and explicitly
  confirmed by the visitor.

### Formal service pages

Each formal service page follows:

1. buyer decision or outcome context;
2. what the service is intended to address;
3. scope and explicit exclusions;
4. approach and dependencies;
5. evidence, demo, expert, insight, and trust links by verified type;
6. sector relevance only when reviewed;
7. limitations and evidence freshness; and
8. discovery CTA.

## Industry hierarchy

The retained `/industries` hub explains focus and evidence policy, then links to
Agriculture and Mining. Each industry page begins with reviewed sector context,
not a generic claim that Hengshi “transforms” the industry. It then maps relevant
services, evidence, trust considerations, and expert-reviewed insight. Sector
pages must not duplicate service pages or create unapproved intersections for
search coverage.

## Work, Demos, and proof language

### Work label

Use **Verified Work** only when the entry satisfies the claim ledger. Each detail
page states the client/public context permitted for disclosure, Hengshi's exact
contribution, evidence basis, limitations, authorization, and dates.

### Demo label

Use **Hengshi-owned capability demonstration** on collection cards, detail pages,
metadata, accessible labels, structured data, and optional 3D panels. State what
is demonstrated, under which conditions, with which data/assets, and what is not
proven.

### Insight label

Use **Expert-reviewed insight** only with a verified named reviewer and original
value. Authorship, review, and evidence roles should not be collapsed if the
publication workflow requires separation.

## Semantic and immersive expression

The message hierarchy is identical in meaning across responsive Quick Access and
the optional 3D campus. The immersive layer may alter pacing, spatial grouping,
and sensory emphasis but may not:

- remove detail or actions from the semantic route;
- use a metaphor as the only explanation of service structure;
- hide formal service titles behind room signs;
- make raycasting, sound, motion, or a powerful device necessary;
- create separate indexable duplicates; or
- present unapproved proof in a room or AI panel.

## Message acceptance checklist

Before exact route copy is approved, confirm that it:

- uses Hengshi Design and the exact formal taxonomy consistently;
- states intended offer versus verified performance accurately;
- maps the route to one dominant buyer question;
- names proof type and limitations visibly;
- preserves agriculture/mining order and defense quarantine;
- offers a semantic, keyboard, reduced-motion, non-WebGL route;
- avoids a fixed price or binding scope/timeline/outcome;
- uses proportionate CTA language; and
- can be understood without animation, imagery, audio, canvas, or jargon.
