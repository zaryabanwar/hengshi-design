# Audience and Buying Context

## Evidence boundary

The accepted foundation defines the audience but records no approved buyer
interviews, quotations, search-demand dataset, win/loss evidence, budget bands,
persona rankings, or market-size estimate. The context below separates approved
facts from working hypotheses. Hypotheses guide later research and must not be
published as buyer claims.

## Approved primary audience

Hengshi Design is intended for global English-speaking mid-market and enterprise
leaders responsible for one or more of:

- technology;
- data;
- product;
- transformation; and
- innovation.

Agriculture is the first sector context and mining is second. Defense is not a
launch audience and remains quarantined. “Global” describes who the offer is
intended to serve; it does not establish offices, customers, coverage, staffing,
regulatory reach, or round-the-clock support.

## Decision context

The approved offer is a custom strategy-to-delivery program, not a fixed package.
The buying decision is therefore expected to involve more than inspiration or a
service list. A useful brand must help a prospective buyer answer four questions:

1. Is this relevant to the outcome and operating context I own?
2. Can I understand the proposed scope, boundaries, and delivery approach?
3. What verified proof, demonstrated capability, expertise, and trust evidence is
   available?
4. Can I reach an appropriate next conversation without disclosing unnecessary
   information or navigating an unfamiliar experience?

These questions are a strategy synthesis of the accepted route, proof, trust, and
booking requirements. They are not findings from buyer interviews.

## Buying-context hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Brand implication | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Leaders evaluating complex change need a concise category frame before a detailed service taxonomy. | Lead with one stable descriptor, then expose exact services and sectors. | Interviews with approved target roles and query-to-route evidence. |
| Enterprise evaluation requires risk, method, ownership, and proof context before or alongside a CTA. | Place proof status, limitations, trust links, and next steps near substantive claims. | Buyer interviews, proof-path use, and qualified-booking feedback. |
| Some buyers will value an authored immersive demonstration, while others need a fast semantic path. | Treat Quick Access and the optional 3D experience as equal expressions of one brand, not premium and inferior tiers. | Accessibility research, path comparison with cohort sizes, and usability evidence. |
| Agriculture and mining audiences need sector-specific problem framing without generic service copy or implied delivery history. | Use sector context to connect relevant services, evidence, and questions; avoid invented domain outcomes. | Named sector reviewers, primary evidence, and approved buyer conversations. |
| A low-friction, direct conversation is more appropriate than fixed public pricing for custom programs. | Use a qualified 30-minute discovery booking as the primary conversion and explain what the conversation will clarify. | Booking research, measured funnel evidence, and founder-approved operating policy. |
| Trust increases when uncertainty and evidence status are visible. | Say what is known, demonstrated, conditional, or unavailable; never fill proof gaps with theatrical confidence. | Qualitative buyer feedback and claim-review outcomes. |

## Audience needs the brand must support

### Rapid orientation

State what Hengshi Design is, who the intended offer serves, and the outcome of a
discovery conversation in plain global English. The home and Quick Access paths
must not require decoding a metaphor or entering WebGL.

### Relevant depth

Allow buyers to move from category to sector, wing, formal service, evidence,
expertise, trust, and booking without losing context. Concision at the top cannot
become vagueness below it.

### Evidence clarity

Distinguish approved offer direction, verified client work, Hengshi-owned demos,
expert-reviewed insight, implemented trust controls, and future intent. Empty or
held proof states must remain honest.

### Safe progression

Let visitors book directly, use optional approved AI, or request human help.
Never imply that AI, live staff, voice/video, a budget, a visitor account, or a
high-end device is required.

## Sector context

### Agriculture first

Agriculture is the first priority for sector-specific content, review capacity,
and evidence development. Messaging may discuss the kinds of technology, data,
product, automation, platform, experience, reliability, and trust questions the
approved services can address. It must not imply existing agricultural customers,
deployments, datasets, credentials, measured outcomes, or universal sector needs.

### Mining second

Mining follows agriculture and uses the same evidence rules. Messaging should
acknowledge operational, reliability, security, data, human, and environmental
context only where reviewed evidence supports the exact statement. It must not
romanticize extraction, generalize across regions or communities, or imply a
delivery record.

### Defense quarantined

No public defense audience message, route, example, demo, dataset, campaign, AI
answer, or ordinary contact flow is authorized. MA-011 requires an exact-scope
founder decision plus independent legal and security review before any change.

## Research and validation backlog

Before route copy or identity expression is finalized:

1. interview verified participants across the approved responsibility areas and
   both priority sectors;
2. inventory real phrases from approved discovery calls, proposals, and briefs
   without exposing confidential data;
3. test the category descriptor, promise, and CTA for comprehension rather than
   preference alone;
4. test both semantic and immersive entry paths with keyboard, screen-reader,
   reduced-motion, low-power, and time-constrained participants;
5. validate sector problem framing with named agriculture and mining reviewers;
6. compare qualitative feedback with qualified-booking and correction evidence
   after launch; and
7. revise hypotheses without silently changing the approved audience, sectors,
   services, proof rules, or conversion model.
