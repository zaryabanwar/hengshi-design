# Naming, Claims, and Terminology Rules

## Public name

The public display name is **Hengshi Design**. Use the full name for entity-
defining headings, metadata, structured data, bylines, credits, approved profiles,
and contexts where “Hengshi” could be confused with an unrelated organization.
The name is approved direction, not a verified legal name or trademark clearance.
Any naming change requires founder and applicable legal approval.

## Terminology rules

### Evidence-led

Use to describe the strategic method: decisions and messages are tied to approved
sources, proof types, and limitations. Do not use it as a blanket assertion that
every current capability, page, or answer has been independently proven.

### Premium

Use primarily as an internal quality standard. Public expression should come
through clarity, craft, accessibility, responsiveness, evidence, and service—not
through “premium,” “elite,” or luxury superiority language without a useful
meaning.

### Innovation

Use for the work of framing and delivering meaningful change. Do not make it a
novelty claim or synonym for new technology. Avoid “revolutionary,” “disruptive,”
and “future-proof.”

### Platform-oriented

Use internally or with explanation to mean connected capabilities, shared truth,
and reusable operating foundations. Do not imply a commercial Hengshi SaaS
platform, marketplace, subscription, or proprietary product unless separately
approved and evidenced.

### Strategy-to-delivery

This is the approved engagement model. It may describe the span Hengshi intends
to support through custom discovery and proposal. Do not turn it into “complete
end-to-end delivery” or imply every service, phase, outcome, timeline, or provider
is included.

### Enterprise

Use to describe the intended audience, relevant scale/complexity context, or an
approved service characteristic. It is not evidence of enterprise clients,
enterprise-grade assurance, global scale, or procurement readiness.

### Global

Use only for the intended English-speaking audience. Do not infer offices, legal
entities, customers, local teams, time-zone coverage, regulatory coverage,
languages, or 24/7 support.

### Transformation

Attach it to a defined decision, system, capability, or operating change. Avoid
“transform your business” as an unsupported universal outcome.

### Agentic automation

Use the exact formal service title **AI Systems & Agentic Automation**. Any detail
about autonomy, agents, accuracy, safety, production readiness, data handling, or
results requires technical evidence and AI/security review.

### Immersive

Use for optional spatial or sensory modes that add decision value. Do not equate
immersion with WebGL alone or imply that an accessible semantic route is lesser.

### Accessible

Describe a requirement, design approach, or tested conformance state precisely.
Do not claim “fully accessible” or WCAG conformance until independent evidence
supports the exact scope, version, date, and exceptions.

## Prohibited public claims

The following remain prohibited unless the exact wording receives the stated
substantiation and approval. Several have no current evidence and should be
treated as unavailable, not merely awaiting better copy.

| Claim class | Prohibited examples | Why held | Release condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Novelty or superiority | world-first; first ever; unique; unrivalled; best; leading; top; most advanced | No defined comparison set or independent substantiation | Exact evidence, comparison scope, independent review, legal clearance, founder approval |
| Client and social proof | trusted by; client logos; testimonials; customer count; partners; awards; ratings | None verified in the accepted ledger | Authorization, evidence, rights, claim review, founder approval |
| Outcomes and performance | increased revenue; reduced cost; faster by; transformed; guaranteed ROI; current capacity | No verified outcome or measurement method | Exact source, method, baseline, period, scope, consent, review |
| Geographic/operating footprint | Atlanta HQ; global offices; worldwide team; 24/7 support | Prototype labels and audience target are not operating facts | Verified legal/operating facts and approved disclosure |
| Trust and assurance | secure; compliant; certified; audited; penetration tested; guaranteed privacy; 99.9% SLA achieved | Requirements and targets are not current assurance | Implemented controls, evidence, independent/legal review, scope/date |

| Sector history | agriculture experts; mining leaders; proven sector delivery | Priorities and service intersections are not history | Named reviewer/credentials or verified work and exact approved wording |

## Conditional claim grammar

Use language that matches evidence state:

| Evidence state | Preferred grammar | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Approved offer direction | “Hengshi Design offers…” after content approval; “is intended to…” in internal drafts | “has delivered…” |
| Proposed design/architecture | “is designed to…” or “the approved requirement is…” | “provides,” “ensures,” “guarantees” |
| Hengshi-owned demo | “demonstrates X under Y conditions” | “proved for clients,” “production-ready” |
| Verified Work | “Hengshi contributed X, supported by Y evidence” | Broader responsibility or outcome than the source permits |
| Target | “targets,” with scope and measurement method | “achieves,” “meets,” “SLA” |
| Missing evidence | “cannot verify,” “not currently available,” or omit | Confident filler, anonymous authority, implied proof |

## Claim review questions

Before a substantive phrase enters copy, metadata, schema, AI knowledge, a 3D
panel, or a CTA, reviewers must be able to answer:

1. Is it an intended offer, demonstrated capability, verified delivery fact,
   measured outcome, implemented control, or independent assurance?
2. What source supports each material part of the sentence?
3. Is the scope, date, comparison set, and limitation visible where needed?
4. Do rights, consent, owner, reviewer, and founder approval exist?
5. Does the same meaning appear consistently in semantic, immersive, structured,
   AI, and social representations?
6. Could the wording imply a customer, location, person, certification, outcome,
   availability, price, or commitment that the evidence does not support?

If any required answer is missing, the claim remains held or is rewritten to the
verified state. Publication pressure is not evidence.
