# Phase 1 Market, Reference, and SEO Evidence

**Project:** Hengshi Design

**Document status:** Phase 1 evidence input; not a final positioning, keyword-priority, route-priority, legal, or crawler-policy decision

**Research snapshot:** 2026-07-19 (Asia/Karachi)

**Audience in scope:** Global English-speaking mid-market and enterprise technology, data, product, transformation, and innovation buyers

**Sector order supplied by the founder:** Agriculture first; mining second

**Primary conversion supplied by the founder:** A qualified 30-minute discovery booking

**Experience constraint supplied by the founder:** Semantic Quick Access must remain equivalent to, and independent of, optional WebGL exploration

## 1. Research question and decision supported

**Research question:** Which observable patterns in the founder-provided reference set and current primary search guidance should inform Hengshi Design's Phase 1 SEO, entity, search-intent, proof, and experience definition, and which matters must remain hypotheses or approval gates?

This document supports later specialist and founder decisions about differentiation, information architecture, content priorities, entity clarity, crawler controls, and measurement. It does not approve public claims, select final keywords, authorize new routes, or turn any reference-site feature into a Hengshi Design requirement.

The evidence labels used throughout are:

- **Observation:** Directly visible on a cited public page or stated in official documentation on the research date.
- **Inference:** A bounded interpretation of one or more observations.
- **Hypothesis:** A proposition that still needs validation through approved buyer evidence, search reporting, or specialist review.
- **Existing decision:** A project instruction already approved outside this research. It is context, not a research finding.

## 2. Method, boundaries, and confidence

### 2.1 Method

The work used four evidence streams:

1. **Founder-supplied reference review.** The public home page and at least one supporting page or explicit alternative experience were inspected for each reference. The same comparison dimensions were used for all four.
2. **Initial-response and rendered-page inspection.** Public HTML responses were checked for titles, descriptions, canonicals, headings, landmarks, links, and canvas use. JavaScript-heavy pages were also inspected after rendering in a real browser. This distinguishes what is present in the initial response from what becomes available after JavaScript.
3. **Primary search guidance.** Google Search Central, Google crawler documentation, W3C/WAI, Schema.org, OpenAI crawler documentation, IndexNow, Bing Webmaster documentation, and web.dev were used for current implementation and reporting guidance.
4. **Entity-collision sampling.** A limited public search and official-site review checked whether other organizations use the Hengshi name in adjacent or unrelated categories.

All public pages and living documentation in this report were accessed on **2026-07-19**. Vendor claims are recorded only as statements the vendor publishes; they were not independently verified. No forms were submitted, no organization was contacted, no account or paid report was used, and no traffic, keyword-volume, authority, or market-share estimate was invented.

### 2.2 Confidence

- **High confidence:** The cited official search guidance and the directly observed page elements in the inspected snapshot.
- **Moderate confidence:** Cross-reference patterns derived from the four inspected sites.
- **Low-to-moderate confidence:** Search-intent and keyword hypotheses, because no approved buyer interviews, search-console data, paid keyword dataset, or current Hengshi Design query history was available.

### 2.3 Material limitations

- The four sites were selected by the founder as creative references. They are not a representative sample of the global technology-services market.
- This was a page-level snapshot, not a site-wide technical crawl, accessibility conformance audit, backlink study, performance audit, source-code review, or commercial due-diligence exercise.
- Dynamic pages can change after the research date. A signal described as “not observed” is not proof that it never appears in another route, state, response variant, or later release.
- Search engines decide whether and how to crawl, canonicalize, index, and display a page. Following guidance improves clarity but does not guarantee indexing, rankings, rich results, or inclusion in an AI answer.
- Legal identity, trademark clearance, public organization details, verified people, proof, and public claims remain manual approval dependencies.

## 3. Approved context kept separate from research

The following are **existing project decisions**, not findings produced by competitor observation:

- Hengshi Design is to be positioned as an evidence-led, premium, platform-oriented innovation delivery company.
- The initial buyer group is global English-speaking mid-market and enterprise leadership across technology, data, product, transformation, and innovation.
- Agriculture leads sector content; mining follows.
- The primary conversion is a qualified discovery booking rather than a published fixed-price purchase.
- Verified client work and clearly labeled Hengshi-owned capability demonstrations must be separate collections.
- Unsupported superiority, “world-first,” customer, award, geographic, outcome, or expertise claims cannot be published.
- Every indexable route must provide complete semantic HTML before JavaScript, and Quick Access must provide a non-WebGL route to the same content and actions.
- English is the launch language. Localized URLs and `hreflang` are valid only when real translations exist.

Research below may support how those decisions are executed, but cannot silently alter them.

## 4. Reference-set classification

|---|---|---|---|

**Inference:** Hengshi Design needs two coordinated systems: the information, proof, trust, and conversion clarity expected in enterprise services; and the memorable authored craft expected from an immersive studio. Neither system should make the other inaccessible, slow, vague, or unindexable.

### 5.2 ScienceSoft evidence notes

**Observation:** The [ScienceSoft home page](https://www.scnsoft.com/) presents software consulting and development, then expands into facts, offerings, industries, solutions, success stories, experts, clients, and company-confidence material. The inspected response exposed conventional document landmarks, a self-canonical, crawlable links, and structured-data graphs. Its [company page](https://www.scnsoft.com/about/company) adds organization history, collaboration, geography, industries, clients, service mapping, pricing context, and call-oriented prompts. Its public [case-study collection](https://www.scnsoft.com/case-studies/all) and [software-development service area](https://www.scnsoft.com/software-development/services) demonstrate separate proof and service taxonomies.

**Inference:** Enterprise buyers benefit when service explanation, relevant proof, expertise, trust, and a next action are adjacent but not collapsed into one generic landing page.

**Hypothesis for Hengshi Design:** Each service and sector page should provide a complete decision unit: buyer problem, outcome sought, scope and boundaries, approach, related verified proof or labeled demo, named reviewed expertise when available, trust considerations, related insight, and discovery-booking path. The quantity and depth must follow verified evidence, not the reference's page count.

### 5.3 Lusion evidence notes

**Observation:** [Lusion](https://lusion.co/) leads with a concise promise around 3D visual storytelling and interactive web experiences, then foregrounds selected projects. Its [about page](https://lusion.co/about/) describes a distributed mix of design, motion, 3D, technology, strategy, creative, and production expertise. The representative [Oryzo AI project](https://lusion.co/projects/oryzo_ai) is explicitly described as self-initiated and identifies the involved disciplines, including concept, web design, development, 3D, WebGL, and animation.

**Inference:** A capability demonstration is more credible when its ownership and status are explicit, the disciplines are named, and the artifact is allowed to show the craft rather than being disguised as client work.

**Hypothesis for Hengshi Design:** The Demos collection should state “Hengshi-owned capability demonstration” prominently, explain the problem being explored, list the demonstrated capabilities and limitations, identify synthetic or public data, and avoid implied client outcomes. This aligns creative proof with the project's existing proof-integrity rule.

### 5.4 Bruno Simon evidence notes

**Observation:** The main [Bruno Simon experience](https://bruno-simon.com/) is an interactive canvas portfolio with visible audio, quality, reset, renderer, and recovery options. A separate [HTML version](https://bruno-simon.com/html/) exposes identity, activities, projects, contact routes, and a link back to the 3D version without requiring canvas interaction.

**Inference:** A memorable immersive experience and a direct content route can coexist as first-class choices. Recovery controls also reduce the cost of an unfamiliar interaction model.

**Hypothesis for Hengshi Design:** Reception should offer Quick Access at the first meaningful choice, retain persistent audio/quality/exit/recovery controls, and keep all service, proof, trust, and booking content reachable without WebGL. The already approved directed point-and-click model remains more appropriate than copying a drive-around or keyboard-game interaction.

### 5.5 Active Theory evidence notes

**Observation:** [Active Theory](https://activetheory.net/) describes itself through creative digital experiences and presents an authored canvas-led interface. The representative [World Brush project](https://activetheory.net/work/world-brush) exposes a project title, category/year context, a concise summary, project navigation, and a link to longer external case-study material after rendering. Its [XR experiments area](https://xr.activetheory.net/) documents experimental and research-oriented work separately from core commercial presentation.

**Inference:** Technical experimentation can strengthen perceived craft when it is clearly separated from the promise made to a buyer and when selected work still has a concise narrative.

**Hypothesis for Hengshi Design:** WebGPU, new interaction techniques, experimental shaders, and research prototypes should remain labeled experiments until they meet the project's production evidence gates. Public work detail should still provide a stable semantic summary independent of the showcase layer.

## 6. Cross-reference findings and implications

### Finding 1: Enterprise clarity and immersive memorability are complementary

**Observation:** ScienceSoft emphasizes taxonomy and proof depth; Lusion, Bruno Simon, and Active Theory emphasize authored interaction and visible craft.

**Inference:** The relevant differentiation opportunity is not “more 3D” or “more pages.” It is a coherent handoff between a fast semantic decision journey and an optional immersive proof experience.

**Implication:** Quick Access should not be framed as a fallback of lesser value. It is the canonical search, accessibility, low-power, and time-constrained buyer experience. WebGL can add orientation, atmosphere, and demonstration, while linking to the same canonical content and actions.

### Finding 2: Proof classification is a strategic trust signal

**Observation:** The references use several kinds of proof: client work, company facts, awards, named projects, self-initiated work, experiments, public code, talks, and experts.

**Inference:** Combining unlike proof into one undifferentiated “work” feed would make it harder for a buyer or search system to understand what actually happened for a client.

**Implication:** Hengshi Design should preserve visible content types and provenance: verified client work, Hengshi-owned demos, expert-reviewed insights, and experimental research. Each type needs distinct labels, eligibility rules, structured data, and approval evidence.

### Finding 3: A concise proposition needs supporting specificity

**Observation:** The specialist studios use short propositions and selected work; the enterprise-services reference adds extensive service, industry, expertise, and trust detail.

**Inference:** A concise home-page promise can attract attention, but an enterprise buyer still needs evidence of relevance, method, risk control, and accountable expertise before booking.

**Implication:** The home page should route quickly to sector, service, proof, expert, and trust evidence. It should not attempt to carry every detail or make the atrium the only navigation model.

### Finding 4: Resilience is part of the experience, not an afterthought

**Observation:** Bruno Simon visibly provides quality and recovery controls and maintains a separate HTML experience. The other immersive references rely more heavily on canvas and JavaScript in the inspected states.

**Inference:** Hengshi Design can make resilience part of its premium signal by designing for reduced motion, low power, failed assets, unsupported WebGL, keyboard navigation, and quick task completion from the outset.

**Implication:** Equivalent semantic journeys, authored recovery states, and transparent controls should be reviewed as product acceptance criteria, not merely technical fallbacks.

## 7. Primary SEO and entity guidance

### 7.1 Semantic HTML and JavaScript

**Observation from official guidance:** Google documents a crawl-render-index pipeline for JavaScript and states that server-side or pre-rendering remains useful because it is faster for users and crawlers and because not every bot executes JavaScript. It recommends meaningful HTTP status codes, crawlable `<a href>` links, unique titles and descriptions, visible text, structured data, and stable canonical handling. [Google JavaScript SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics)

W3C/WAI explains that regions, logical headings, and structured page sections help people understand and navigate content. [WAI page-structure guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/page-structure/)

**Inference:** Complete initial HTML is valuable for accessibility, reliability, sharing, agent interpretation, and buyer speed in addition to search discovery.

**Hengshi implication:** Every indexable page should return its meaningful title, description, canonical, H1, headings, landmarks, content, links, breadcrumbs, and truthful JSON-LD without waiting for the WebGL scene or client application. The immersive room should link to the same canonical service document; it should not create a duplicate indexable version of that document.

### 7.2 Canonicals, duplicates, status codes, and internal links

**Observation from official guidance:** Google treats redirects, `rel="canonical"`, and sitemap inclusion as canonicalization signals, recommends consistent internal linking to the preferred URL, and describes canonical selection as a preference rather than a guarantee. It also advises placing canonical information in the HTML source and avoiding conflicting signals. [Canonicalization overview](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization) and [duplicate-URL consolidation guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls)

**Hengshi implication:** The approved host, lowercase hyphenated paths, no-trailing-slash rule, redirects, sitemap entries, internal links, Open Graph URLs, and structured-data URLs should agree. Retired content needs deliberate `301`, `404`, or `410` behavior rather than a successful page that merely says “not found.” Filter, session, preview, admin, staging, and unpublished states must not generate competing canonical pages.

### 7.3 Structured data and entity consistency

**Observation from official guidance:** Google recommends JSON-LD and requires structured data to be representative of the visible page, current, relevant, and non-misleading. Correct markup does not guarantee a rich result. [Structured-data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies)

Google's official documentation describes organization details such as name, URL, logo, and verified profiles; `WebSite` markup supports a preferred site name; breadcrumb markup communicates hierarchy; and article markup can connect content to identifiable authors and dates. [Organization](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization), [site name](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names), [breadcrumbs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/breadcrumb), and [article](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article) guidance. Schema.org defines the relevant vocabularies, including [Organization](https://schema.org/Organization), [Service](https://schema.org/Service), [Article](https://schema.org/Article), and [BreadcrumbList](https://schema.org/BreadcrumbList).

**Hengshi implication:** Use “Hengshi Design” consistently in visible copy, metadata, `Organization`, and home-page `WebSite` data. Populate legal name, logo, people, addresses, contact points, and `sameAs` only after verification. A service, person, article, image, or video graph must match an actual visible entity on that page. Reviews, ratings, prices, customers, awards, locations, outcomes, and authors cannot be inferred into markup.

### 7.4 People-first and AI-assisted publishing

**Observation from official guidance:** Google asks whether content has original information, useful analysis, clear sourcing, identifiable expertise, and a people-first purpose. Its generative-AI guidance allows AI assistance but warns that scaled pages offering little value can violate spam policies and emphasizes accuracy, quality, relevance, and correct metadata. [People-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) and [generative-AI content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)

**Hengshi implication:** Every public insight needs a defined buyer question, named accountable reviewer, primary sources, original Hengshi analysis or evidence, claim review, and approval date. AI-supported drafts must not bypass expert verification. Service-sector intersection pages should exist only when Hengshi has enough differentiated evidence to answer a real question; route multiplication is not a content strategy.

### 7.5 Robots, indexing controls, and AI crawler controls

**Observation from official guidance:** Google states that a crawler must be allowed to fetch a page to see a page-level `noindex` directive. A `robots.txt` disallow controls crawling but is not, by itself, a reliable method for keeping a known URL out of search; access control or `noindex` is needed according to the use case. [Robots meta guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag), [Search essentials introduction](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/get-started), and [robots.txt specification](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec)

Google documents `Google-Extended` as a control for certain Gemini training and grounding uses and says it does not affect Google Search inclusion or ranking. [Google crawler token documentation](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers)

OpenAI distinguishes `OAI-SearchBot`, used for search discovery and result links, from `GPTBot`, used to identify content that may be used for model training; the controls can be set independently. [OpenAI crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) and [publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)

The same OpenAI documentation describes `ChatGPT-User` as a user-triggered fetcher rather than an automatic search crawler and states that `robots.txt` rules may not apply to those user-initiated requests. Google's documentation describes `Google-Extended` as a product token rather than a separate request user agent and connects the one control to more than one documented Gemini model-use purpose.

**Inference:** “Allow answer/search discovery, block training-only use unless approved” is technically expressible for some providers, but not as one universal binary rule. Automatic search, training, user-triggered fetching, and mixed-purpose tokens have different enforcement properties; some provider uses are not separable under the controls currently exposed.

**Hengshi implication:** Maintain a dated crawler registry that separates automatic search/answer crawlers, training crawlers, user-triggered fetchers, mixed-purpose tokens, and unknown automation. Record request identity or token, documented purpose, whether robots rules apply, whether purposes are technically separable, official source/date, proposed policy, owner, and review date. `ChatGPT-User` cannot be governed as if it were `OAI-SearchBot`; `Google-Extended` cannot be documented as separate training and grounding switches while the provider exposes one coupled token. Drafts, previews, private content, and admin systems require authentication and publication isolation regardless of crawler identity. Exact production policy requires founder, legal, security, and privacy approval and quarterly review.

### 7.6 Performance measurement

**Observation from official guidance:** The current Core Web Vitals thresholds classifying a good experience are LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 milliseconds, and CLS at or below 0.1, assessed at the 75th percentile of real-user page loads. [Core Web Vitals threshold methodology](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)

**Hengshi implication:** Measure the semantic Quick Access journey independently from lazy 3D loading. Field data at the 75th percentile is the outcome measure; laboratory tests are release diagnostics. Reporting should segment route class, device class, and experience mode where privacy-preserving aggregation supports it.

### 7.7 Search notification and reporting

**Observation from official guidance:** IndexNow notifies participating search engines about changed URLs, but a successful submission response confirms receipt rather than indexing. [IndexNow documentation](https://www.indexnow.org/documentation)

Bing Webmaster documentation exposes search, crawl, link, keyword, sitemap, and URL-submission capabilities after site ownership and account setup. [Bing Webmaster API documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/bingwebmaster/)

Google Search Console's performance reporting includes clicks, impressions, click-through rate, average position, queries, pages, countries, and devices. Google also documents branded/non-branded analysis and cautions that report data is truncated or anonymized and commonly attributed to canonical URLs. [Performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553), [branded-query analysis](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17010961), and [data caveats](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17011259)

**Hengshi implication:** Search notification is an operational action, not proof of discovery. A useful launch dashboard should distinguish:

- **Technical availability:** valid indexable HTML, canonical and status-code correctness, sitemap processing, crawl and index coverage, structured-data validity, and field Core Web Vitals.
- **Discovery:** non-branded and branded impressions, clicks, click-through rate, query cluster, landing page, country, and device, interpreted with platform data limits.
- **Buyer progress:** aggregate transitions from organic landing to relevant service/industry/proof content, booking start, verified booking, and qualified booking.
- **Entity clarity:** impressions and clicks for “Hengshi Design” and close variants, search-result title/site-name consistency, and accidental association with unrelated Hengshi entities.

The primary business measure should be qualified organic discovery bookings, supported by diagnostic measures. Average position alone is not a business outcome.

## 10. Evidence-led content implications

### 10.1 Minimum credible content atom

**Hypothesis:** A strong indexable service, sector, work, demo, insight, expert, or trust page should contain:

- a clear page purpose and one truthful H1;
- the buyer question or outcome it addresses;
- a concise answer visible in initial HTML;
- verified scope, method, limitations, and evidence appropriate to the content type;
- named ownership or expert review when applicable;
- provenance for claims, images, data, and demonstrations;
- related proof and internal links that are useful rather than mechanically inserted;
- a next action appropriate to the buyer's stage;
