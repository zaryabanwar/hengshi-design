Accepted movement scope · proposed control details

Walk through the place, rather than orbit its centre.

The production experience puts you at eye level on connected ground. You can look around, steer freely, or choose a destination. The current campus viewer is an inspection model: its orbit and zoom controls do not demonstrate this walking experience.

Desktop proposal

Mouse: look, move and stop

Drag empty scene space to look around. An explicit Move action starts travel in your chosen direction; turning your view steers that travel along the ground. Stop remains available. Looking up does not make you fly.

Click an eligible floor point for a safe directed route. Clicking a department board opens its information instead. Dragging does not accidentally click a destination.

Touch proposal

Touch: the same freedom

A scene drag changes the view. An optional thumb pad can move you while engaged, stopping on release or cancellation. Its exact gesture and cancellation design still needs the approved screen and accessibility inspection.

Move, Stop, directions, Look and destination actions also offer a one-finger option without dragging or using two fingers together. Keyboard-accessible actions remain available; WASD is not required.

Load nearby areas without losing the ground beneath you.

  1. Keep the current groundThe place supporting you and its collision data stay ready and protected. A visible building alone does not make its floor safe to enter.
  2. Prepare what comes nextLoad eligible nearby areas within the selected stream's limits. A turn changes priorities; it does not require downloading the whole landscape.
  3. Handle a sudden reversalRetain the required adjacent ground and a recent-area buffer. If the return path is not ready, stop at the last safe position while it prepares.
  4. Hold safely and recoverAt an unready edge, show truthful waiting/retry and equivalent website access. Do not walk into missing ground, jump to another room or silently change graphics stream.
Reading a board stops travel. Opening information, help or booking clears movement. So do input cancellation, leaving the World focus area, a hidden tab, graphics loss or Exit. Closing a panel or returning to the tab never restarts old travel automatically. Quick Access keeps the same core information and actions without 3D.
Engineering targets and how we will verify them

A consistent human scale

The starting proposal is an upright 1.70 m body, a 1.60 m eye height and a maximum 1.20 m/s walking speed. Authored public passages target at least 1.20 m clear width and 2.10 m clear height, with smooth routes and no automatic stair climbing.

These are unmeasured virtual design targets, not physical building-code standards. The actual model's scale, corners, headroom, connected floors and collision still need verification.

A smooth-looking desktop screenshot or orbit viewer cannot establish walking safety, mobile comfort, accessibility, thermal behavior or graphics suitability. Reduced motion removes decorative camera drift/bob and animated destination travel; explicit destination changes still need ready ground.

Three possible results for each observed minute

Good

There is affirmative, sufficient evidence for every required check. No observation, no traffic or an old successful check cannot silently count as good.

Bad

A valid service failure was observed. Planned maintenance and outages in required providers remain counted, even when the system handles them safely.

Unknown

Evidence is missing, late or insufficient. Unknown time remains in the month and cannot be reported as uptime. An observed failure still counts as bad.

Use calendar months in UTC. The proposed report shows good minutes / all minutes, the possible upper bound including unknown minutes, and measurement coverage. Only the conservative lower bound can establish the target. This is a sampled measure; it does not prove continuous service between checks.

What the recovery targets mean after a serious outage

Phase 1 engineering proposal Two accepted targets describe different things: at most 24 hours of data-loss exposure and at most eight hours to recover service. Neither has been demonstrated. Their launch-risk reassessment remains open.

How much committed data can be recovered?

Validate a complete, consistent recovery point for the required durable records and compatible artifacts. Measure the worst possible gap before the interruption. A recent backup filename or one newest record does not prove the rest is recoverable.

When does service actually recover?

The clock includes detection, response, restoration and verification. Every affected required boundary must have current passing evidence. A working homepage or database does not prove booking, private API actions or staff access have recovered.

  1. Establish the interruptionUse evidence of when service became unavailable or unsafe. Starting the repair later does not restart the clock.
  2. Restore in quarantineRecover coherent authoritative records and eligible artifacts. Keep old workers, provider writes and private serving fenced until their authority is verified.
  3. Apply current safeguardsEnforce deletion, expiry, withdrawn consent/content and revoked access before serving. Restoring a backup cannot resurrect them or reset their deadlines.
  4. Verify all affected boundariesOld passes cease to count if a boundary fails again. Recheck it without resetting the original outage start, then verify safe routing and current operations.

For an uncertain outage start, use the earliest plausible start to measure recovery duration and the latest plausible start to measure data-loss exposure. For example, an outage beginning between midnight and 02:00 with a consistent recovery point at 01:00 the previous day gives a possible 23 to 25 hours of exposure. The 25-hour worst case misses the 24-hour target. This is arithmetic, not a real recovery drill.

Some individual provider operations may remain truthfully pending after service resumes, provided their authority, fencing and reconciliation work. Recovery must not repeat an unknown booking effect or invent a confirmation. This does not excuse a continuing service outage or missing operation history. Lost ephemeral conversations are not reconstructed as durable records.
